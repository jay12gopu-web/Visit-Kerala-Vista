Palakkad Rainforest

Silent Valley National Park

A protected Western Ghats rainforest known for extraordinary biodiversity, the Kunthipuzha River and a landmark conservation story.

Why Silent Valley

One of Kerala's most carefully protected forest landscapes.

Silent Valley forms part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve and protects tropical evergreen rainforest in Palakkad district. It is especially associated with the endangered lion-tailed macaque, rich birdlife and the successful public movement that helped safeguard the valley.

Rainforest Highlights

What To Experience At Silent Valley

Rainforest

Evergreen Forest

Experience the layered Western Ghats canopy through access permitted by the park, without treating the protected core as an unrestricted trail.

River

Kunthipuzha Landscape

The river runs through the valley's forest ecosystem and forms one of its defining natural features.

Conservation

Wildlife & Forest Learning

Guided interpretation can introduce endemic plants, birds and primates, but no sighting is guaranteed and visitors must keep a respectful distance.

Conservation First

A national park shaped by public protection.

The campaign against a proposed hydroelectric project became a defining Indian conservation movement. The national park was declared in 1984, and access today remains controlled to protect a fragile rainforest ecosystem.

Official Silent Valley National Park
Visit Responsibly

Let park guidance set the day.

  • Confirm permits, reporting point and authorised programme
  • Expect weather or fire-risk closures and changing access
  • Use authorised guides and stay on permitted routes
  • Do not feed wildlife, play loud audio or leave litter
Build It In

Give the park its own day from Mukkali.

Silent Valley is not included in the six published itineraries. Use Mukkali or Mannarkkad as a practical base, confirm the official programme first and avoid combining the visit with a long onward transfer on the same day.
